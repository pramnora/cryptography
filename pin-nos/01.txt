1.1 - USE 4 DIGIT BIRTH DATE(FORWARDS/REVERSED/RANDOMISED)

A lot of debit card/credit cards use a PIN nos...; 
which is a combination of 4 numbers;.
and, where any single digit can range going from: 0-9.

The simplest easiest -laziest- quickest to remember...
-(which one is very unlikely to forget...?!)-
4 letter pin no. is, most probably, one's own birth date.
In my own case, I was born in: 1963.

Now, the problem there is...an intelligent hacker can guess that...???
All they have to do is presume you are NOT more than 100 years old.
Then, starting with what is the current year, now: 2026...subtract 100:-

 2026
- 100
-----
 1926

So, at least, in theory...they just have to keep on, continuously, guessing by typing in a series of 4 numbers progressing from 1926;
then, just repeatedly, add +1:
1926
1927 
1928
...until when they reach 1963...; and, there, they've now cracked it!

NOTE(1): A simple variation is to use your birth year only reversed: 1963 -> 3691.
(Which, I guess, would be that much more difficult to guess at...;
 unless the hacker were to try typing in the birth date numbers going in reverse.)

NOTE(2): Another solution would be to mix up the numbers...so, they no longer go logically: 1963(forwards)/3691(backwards)...;
6193/or, 9316/-etc.
...your only difficulty, now, is...; to remember what was the exact order of re-arrangement...???

NOTE(3): I believe that most web sites/banks/-etc. do already know about this repeated guessing trick...;
so, they put in place steps to deal with it.

A. The user is given only give 3 tries, alone...; after which they have to request a brand 'new' PIN nos., instead.
B. They set an extended time limit...; so, if the 3 guesses are all 'wrong'...; then, they are advised to try, again, later in say 20 minutes time.

1.2.1 - LOGIC(1)/MATHS BASED (USE OPPOSITES: ADD/SUBTRACT...MULTIPLY/DIVIDE)

Step 1: Start with: Addition...; then, do its opposite, which is: Subtract
Step 2: Next, change to doing 'fast' Addition: Multiply...; then, do its opposite, which is: Divide.

Calculation Formula:-

n=3

n+n=6
n-n=0 NOTE: This will always be 0 (eg. any number - itself = 0)
n*n=9
n/n=1 NOTE: This will always be 1 (eg. any number dived by 1 = 1)

So, if we remember that both the 2nd/and, last digits will always be = 01;
then, all we have to remember is just 2 digits, alone; both representing 1st and 3rd place values(slow add=add/fast add=multiply); 
and, in this case, where 3 is the number we originally 'started' out with...those 2 numbers will be: 69 = 6(3+3)/9(3*3);
then, when we put a 0 in the middle: 609/and, a 1 at the end, we get: 6091.

1.3 - USE PURELY 'RANDOM' NUMBERS/(AND, MNEMONIC -PICTURES/STORY- TECHNIQUE)

I'm sure, there are many other logic ways that it's possible to make up a 4 digit combination PIN nos.

Including...just select a bunch of entirely 'random' digits inside of your own head:

9467

...now, we need a way to remember each digit, for example:-

9: A lassoo with the circle going on top
4: A ship with it's sail seen pointing left
6: A lassoo with the circle going down
7: A hockey stick

I personally have found that rather than trying to remember numbers by just thinking purely in terms of numbers; then, I tend to forget...?!
But, when I add something else to the number, like a picture/or, even, multiple pictures...;
then, I find numbers so much easier to remember.

It's also good to add in some sort of a story.

The lassoo (9) reaches out grabs a sail boat (4)...; then, pulls that sail boat in...lassoo reverses itself (6)...;
then, when that sail boat has been pulled straight back into shore...by the lassoo;
I wack it straight back out there, again, by using a giant hockey stick(7).

NOTE: The more colourful/and, highly absurd the story is...; 
then, the easier it is to remember/(and, in actual fact, one just is NOT be able to forget such a colourful/and, absurd story...?!).
